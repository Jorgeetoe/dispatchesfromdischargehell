## What Is Telos?

Telos (τέλος) — Greek for *end*, *purpose*, or *ultimate aim*. In ethics and philosophy, it refers to the highest good that a thing or person is meant to achieve.

This publication has one.

## The Thesis

The American inpatient rehabilitation discharge system operates on a set of rules that are internally consistent, economically rational, and almost entirely invisible to the families it affects. This site documents those rules.

Not the rules in the policy manuals. The actual rules — the ones that determine why a patient who can't walk gets discharged on a Thursday, why insurance says "not medically necessary" about care that clearly is, why the case manager who's supposed to help you knows something you don't and isn't saying it.

These patterns repeat. Across facilities, across payers, across decades. They repeat because the incentives that produce them haven't changed. Documenting them is the first step toward families understanding what's actually happening — and the first step toward professionals being able to point at something public and say *this is what I've been trying to explain*.

## The Method

Three principles guide everything published here:

**Documentation over advocacy.** This site is not a reform manifesto. Reform requires political will, and political will requires a public that understands the problem. We're still at the documentation stage. Every post is evidence first, argument second.

**Mechanics over emotion.** The system isn't broken because bad people run it. It's broken because the incentive structures produce predictable, rational behavior that happens to harm patients. Understanding the mechanics — who is incentivized to do what, and why — is more useful than outrage.

**Pattern recognition over anecdote.** Individual stories illustrate. Patterns explain. Every scenario on this site is de-identified and composite, drawn from 20 years of cases, because the point is never "look what happened to this person." The point is "this happens to everyone, and here's why."

## The Audience

This is written for:

- **Case managers and discharge planners** who've spent years solving the same problems and want to see them documented somewhere public
- **Clinicians** who know the system is broken but are too busy living inside it to articulate why
- **Families** who've just learned their loved one is being discharged somewhere they're unprepared for, on a timeline they don't understand, and need someone to explain what's actually happening

This site is not written for anyone who benefits from the current system remaining opaque.

## The Voice

I write like someone who's been in the room. Not like a reformer making a principled argument. Like someone who's sat in 10,000 family conferences, read 50,000 pages of insurance denial language, and watched the same pattern play out across two decades.

Dark humor, operational realism, and specific examples replace inspiration and hope. Hope is for people who haven't yet learned how the system works. This site is for people who need to survive inside it.

## What This Is Not

- A replacement for professional consultation
- A rating system for facilities or insurers
- A legal resource (though legal questions will be discussed)
- A medical advice column
- A policy paper proposing solutions

It is documentation. Mechanics. The rules beneath the surface, written down.
