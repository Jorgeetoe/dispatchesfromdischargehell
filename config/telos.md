# Dispatches from Discharge Hell — Telos

## Why This Exists

The people inside the healthcare system see the problems clearly. We talk about
them constantly—in hallways, in team meetings, in frustrated group chats. But
the people going through the system—patients and families—are left to figure
it out alone, in crisis mode, with no map and no time.

This site is an attempt to change that.

## The Problems I'm Exposing

1. **Insurance games** — Prior authorization delays, denial strategies, network
   manipulation, and the financial incentives that drive coverage decisions

2. **Placement failures** — The gap between what patients need and what the
   system will provide, especially for catastrophic injuries

3. **Family education gaps** — What no one tells families about what's coming,
   and what they need to know to survive the system

4. **Institutional pressures** — Why hospitals push discharge before patients
   are ready, and how the business model conflicts with patient needs

5. **Geographic inequity** — How your ZIP code determines your access to
   specialized care and ongoing support

## What I Believe

- The system isn't broken. It's working exactly as designed. The design is the problem.
- Families can fight back if they know how. Information is leverage.
- Dark humor is a survival mechanism, not disrespect.
- Clinicians inside the system are mostly trying to help. The constraints they
  work under are the enemy.
- Transparency about system mechanics is a form of patient advocacy.

## What Success Looks Like

- A family reads a Field Note and asks the right question at the right moment
- A case manager shares a Machine post with their team and something clicks
- A patient advocate uses a Dispatch to illustrate a pattern to a policymaker
- Someone inside the system feels less alone because someone finally said it out loud

## What This Is Not

- A hospital newsletter
- Medical advice
- Legal advice
- A personal grudge against any individual
- Burnout posting — this is activated, not defeated

## The Voice

I'm not writing as a whistleblower or a victim. I'm writing as a veteran who
knows exactly how this machine works, who has spent 20+ years learning its
patterns, and who believes that knowledge is power.

The tone is: "Let me tell you what's actually happening here."
