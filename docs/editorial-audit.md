# Editorial Audit

This audit borrows selectively from what works on Daniel Miessler's site while keeping this site distinct in voice, subject matter, and visual identity.

## What already works here

- The site already has a strong point of view. It does not read like a generic healthcare blog.
- The category structure is doing useful editorial work: `dispatches`, `field-notes`, `the-machine`, and `when-it-breaks` feel like real lanes instead of filler taxonomy.
- The `Start Here` page already does a job similar to an orientation page on a personal publication site.
- Posts are strongest when they combine one clear claim, one system-level explanation, and one concrete family-facing implication.

## Daniel-style patterns worth borrowing

### 1. Stronger flagship pathways

Daniel's site makes it easy to understand the main ideas quickly. For this site, the equivalent is not copying the homepage layout. It is making sure readers can always find:

- the 25-part series entry point
- the best "how the system works" explainer
- the best "what families should do next" explainer

Recommended content move:

- Keep using `Start Here` as the human entry point.
- Make sure key evergreen posts cross-link each other explicitly in their opening or closing sections.
- Treat a small set of evergreen posts as your core canon and update their excerpts/descriptions carefully.

### 2. More explicit post scaffolding

Daniel's essays often feel easy to follow because the structure is visible early. Your strongest posts already do this informally. Lean into it.

Recommended authoring pattern:

1. Open with the tension or contradiction.
2. Name the system mechanism.
3. Show what families think is happening.
4. Show what is actually happening.
5. End with what the reader should understand, ask, or watch for.
6. Add `Notes` when the post leans on research, policy, or public reporting.

### 3. Notes as trust infrastructure

The new `notes:` field is a good fit for your site because it supports authority without changing your voice into academic prose.

Best use:

- evergreen explainers
- research-backed posts
- posts making broad system claims

Skip notes when:

- the post is primarily reflective or observational
- the citations would add clutter but not clarity

### 4. Better internal canon-building

Daniel's writing benefits from a strong sense that individual posts belong to a larger body of work. You can do that without changing templates.

Recommended content move:

- Add one in-body "If this is your first post here..." or "Related pieces:" line near the top or bottom of cornerstone posts.
- Reuse a consistent trio of internal links across flagship posts.
- Let posts point to category hubs intentionally, not just incidentally.

### 5. Sharper homepage purpose statement

Daniel's homepage quickly answers: who is this for, what am I building, why should you care?

Your site already answers this in `Start Here`, but the homepage itself relies heavily on the post feed.

Without changing the template, the best lever is editorial:

- tighten the homepage-visible excerpts on cornerstone posts
- prioritize post titles that signal mechanism and consequence
- keep the first page of the feed weighted toward evergreen explainers, not just chronology, when possible

## What not to borrow

- Do not borrow the tech-founder / research-lab tone. Your authority comes from bedside-adjacent operational experience and family-facing clarity.
- Do not over-fragment the site into too many nav items. Daniel's site has a broader portfolio; this one should stay publication-first.
- Do not replace your healthcare-system specificity with abstract philosophy. You can widen the frame occasionally, but the engine of the site is still discharge, catastrophic care, and payer logic.

## Recommended next content moves

1. Add `notes:` to research-heavy evergreen posts first.
2. Standardize a stronger closing pattern for flagship posts: one sentence of takeaway, one sentence of what to read next.
3. Build a "core five" list of evergreen posts and keep those excerpts especially sharp.
4. Use `Start Here` as the main onboarding page and keep refining it as the canon grows.
