## The Marketing Promise

The hospital website shows the rehab tower. Lists outcomes. Talks about "comprehensive neuro rehabilitation" and "individualized care plans." Families read about 3-4 month programs. They show up expecting time.

They don't expect to fight insurance at week 2.

## Insurance Reality

Medicare gives 3-4 weeks. Medicaid varies by state but it's similar. Commercial payers? They want to know your discharge plan before you've finished your initial medical stabilization.

The hospital's 3-4 month expectation isn't wrong—it's just not funded.

## The Conversation

Families have a choice nobody tells them about clearly enough:
- Pay out of pocket to stay longer
- Discharge to inadequate placement
- Fight insurance for additional days (usually lose)
- Accept "transitional" placement and never transition back

"Transitional" is a corporate word for "this is probably where they'll stay."

## What Happens In Practice

Census pressure + capital needs + reputation economics = admissions beyond what insurance will fund.

The hospital knows. The payer knows. The family finds out in week 3 when the discharge planner says "let's talk about options."

The options are never as good as the intake coordinator promised.
